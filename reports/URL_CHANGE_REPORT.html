What our URLs are made of, and what the already-decided ones mean. Measured from the WordPress database and the approved decision files. Read-only. 9 September 2026.

1. What a Chimcare URL is made of

Nearly every URL sits under /location/ and names three things: a service, a city and a state.

/location/chimney-sweep-in-seattle-wa/ SERVICE CITY STATE

Checking all 262,987 URLs for how many carry each part:

The URL names…URLsShare
A service262,987100.0%
A state262,83799.9%
A city258,58398.3%
All three together258,54398.3%

The structure is more consistent than it looks. Fewer than 2 in 100 URLs are missing a piece. The problem is not that the pattern is absent, but that a small number break it in expensive ways.

Correct — same thing, same place /location/chimney-sweep-in-st-helens-or/ → /location/chimney-sweep-st-helens-or/ Wrong — different job /location/pellet-stove-service-in-lynn-ma/ → /location/wood-burning-stove-installation-in-lynn-ma/ A pellet stove customer sent to a wood stove installation page. Wrong — different city and state /location/cleveland-chimney-sweep/ → /location/chimney-sweep-portland-oregon/ Cleveland, Ohio sent to Portland, Oregon. 524 clicks.
Why this matters. Google treats a redirect landing on an unrelated page as a broken page and passes none of the value across. 92.8% of approved redirects send the visitor to a different service than the one they searched for. Only 2,210 are a clean like-for-like move.
Good news. The 49,223 different-service redirects are not 49,223 separate decisions. They collapse into 4,403 service pairs. Deciding “does chimney sweep forward to chimney sweep repair?” once fixes hundreds of URLs at a time.

One claim we had to correct. We were told this group had zero Google traffic. Not true: 32,347 have views and 1,972 have clicks. But the amounts are small, and that matters too — 34,787 have no clicks, 1,955 have 1–4, and only 17 have 5 or more. The highest earner has 13 clicks and is a PDF, not a page. Retiring all 36,759 costs about 2,445 clicks out of 75,524 site-wide, roughly 3%. The decision is sound; the reason given for it was wrong.

6. If we change the URL pattern

Proposal: change /location/{service}-in-{city}-{state}/ to /{state}/location/{city}/{service}/. We tested whether it can actually be built.

ResultURLsShare
Can be converted automatically218,32295.1%
Needs a person to decide first11,3184.9%

The 11,318 are the same broken ones from page 1 — duplicate copies, missing cities, editing notes. They carry 1,701 clicks and 522,038 Google views, so they are not throwaway pages. A further 302 collisions would occur, where two or three old URLs become one new URL.

The bigger issue. Changing every URL means every existing redirect points at an address that has itself moved. Redirect rules to maintain go from 53,056 to 282,696 — more than five times as many — and the current set already has problems on 96% of its rows.
Recommendation: change one thing at a time.
  • Step 1 — Move to the new website. Keep every URL exactly as it is.
  • Step 2 — Fix the ~11,000 broken addresses in WordPress.
  • Step 3 — Settle the service list (208 names for 92 services).
  • Step 4 — Then restructure, on a clean base, as its own release.
Steps 2 and 3 are already on the plan. Doing them first turns a 95% conversion into a 100% one.
