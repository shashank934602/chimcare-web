2. How we are doing it

We do not rebuild your pages. We copy them exactly from your live database and prove the copy matches. The method has four rules, and we have not broken any of them.

RuleWhat it means in practice
Copy, never writeNo sentence, image, question, price or address is invented. If your site has it, we copy it word for word. If your site does not have it, the field stays empty and the page is flagged for you
Your site is never touchedWe read from a copy of your database. Nothing we do can change your live website. It stays as the fallback for 30 days after launch
Every page is checked against the originalFifteen automated checks per page: same address, same heading, every question and answer word for word, the identical image file, correct phone number, correct search-engine markup
Moving a page and publishing it are separateEvery page is moved. A page goes live only if it has enough real content. A page that does not is held for your review, never padded to make it pass

Proof: Minnesota

We ran the full method on Minnesota first. 134 city pages were copied. 109 went live. 25 are held because your site does not contain enough local text for them, and we did not write any. All 642 questions and answers moved word for word. All 15 images moved byte for byte. Two full runs produced identical output. Your site was not touched.

4. Your options

These are not four separate projects. They are four answers to the question "what matters most", and each one changes what we build, what it costs, and what you keep.

Option A — SEO first

What we build. Fewer, stronger pages. Every address that already works is kept. The 85,227 pages with no home are consolidated: each one is forwarded to the best related page that exists, and the pages that add nothing are retired. Branch offices get the strongest pages on the site.

You get: the outcome Google now rewards. Since March 2024, Google's rules name "town pages minimally rewritten from a template" as abuse, and a site full of them can drag down its good pages. Fewer pages that are genuinely different rank better than 230,000 copies. Your branch pages, which are worth the most, finally go live.

You give up: around 100,000 addresses stop being pages. Each one forwards somewhere relevant, so nobody hits a dead end, but they are no longer separate pages in Google.

Effort: the largest. What we need from you: a decision on each of the 208 services, and access to Google Search Console so we retire nothing that earns traffic.

Option B — Every address preserved

What we build. One extra page template that takes your existing page content exactly as it is and shows it inside the new site's design. All 229,621 pages stay live at their current addresses. Nothing is consolidated, nothing is retired.

You get: the lowest possible risk on launch day. Every bookmark, every link, every indexed page still works. This is genuinely the safest short-term choice, and it is what you asked about when you said "create it as it is".

You give up: the SEO improvement. You carry the 8,339 duplicates, the 227,511 Boston photographs and the template-page pattern into the new site. Google's assessment of the site as a whole does not change. It is a faster copy of the same problem.

Effort: the smallest. What we need from you: almost nothing. Fix the 30,089 formatting tags on your side, or we handle them in code.

Option C — Content first

What we build. The same verbatim template as Option B, so every page's text moves word for word into the new design, plus the content repairs: the 30,089 formatting tags, the 1,642 pages saved as raw text, real photographs per branch, and the missing search-result descriptions.

You get: every word you have written preserved and readable, presented properly, with the technical fixes that already lift your page scores from 85 to 100 in Google's own tool.

You give up: the structural SEO gain. The page count stays the same, so the duplicate and template problems remain. Better pages, same crowd.

Effort: moderate. What we need from you: photographs, and the formatting fix on your side if you can do it.

Option D — SEO + addresses + content together

What we build. Every address gets exactly one deliberate outcome. Working pages are kept at their address. Content moves word for word into the new design. Weak duplicates are merged into one strong page. Pages with no equivalent forward to the closest relevant page, never to the homepage. Pages with no purpose are retired cleanly. Every forward is checked to land on a real page before launch.

You get: everything Options A, B and C each deliver on their own, without the trade-offs. Nothing valuable is lost, nothing duplicated is carried, every word is preserved, and the site as a whole gets the shape Google rewards.

You give up: speed. This is the longest path, and it cannot finish until you have made the service decisions.

Effort: largest, but it is Option A's work plus Option C's work, not something extra. What we need from you: the service decisions, Search Console access, the redirect confirmations, photographs.

Two specific alternatives you raised

"Create it as it is." That is Option B. It works, it is safe on the day, and it leaves the SEO where it is. If launch date matters more than ranking gain, it is a reasonable first step, and Option D can follow it later without redoing anything, because the addresses never change.

6. What we will not do

  • Forward pages to the homepage. Google treats it as a dead page and it frustrates visitors.
  • Write text, descriptions or captions to fill gaps. What you did not write, the site does not say.
  • Swap the Boston photo for a nicer one in code. The right fix is a real photo in your site, which we then copy.
  • Lower the bar for what goes live. Thin pages pull down good ones.
  • Call any page "thin" until we have measured it. We have not yet.
  • Change an address that works. Keeping a working address is the lowest-risk choice in every source we consulted.
  • Touch your live site. Read only, always.
